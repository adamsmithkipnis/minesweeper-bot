# Hashtag pool.
#
# One tag per line: `#tag [weight] [day]`
#
#   weight   how often it is drawn, relative to 1. Defaults to 1.
#   day      mon..sun — the tag is only eligible on that weekday.
#
# Weights exist because a bigger pool is not automatically more reach. The
# tags here were chosen by reading the public posts of the 63 people who have
# actually played: 18 of them use #gamedev and 12 use #indiedev, while most
# of the pool is used by one or two. Sampling 5 tags uniformly from 232 would
# show #gamedev on one post in 55 instead of one in four — more variety and
# twelve times less reach on the single best tag.
#
# So the weights of the proven tags are roughly three times the number of
# players measured using them, which costs almost nothing in variety:
#
#                        #gamedev    a tag repeats after
#   23 tags, uniform       22.8%        3 posts
#   232 tags, uniform       1.8%       31 posts
#   232 tags, weighted     18.2%       22 posts
#
# The puzzle family (#logicpuzzles, #puzzlegames, #thinkygames...) is the one
# deliberate exception, weighted above what the measurement supports. Only
# one or two players use those tags — but that is a biased sample, because
# they are the people who already found the bot, and they found it through
# the gamedev tags. The puzzle tags are the bet on reaching the other crowd.
#
# Nothing here may be a claim this project cannot make. The audience's most
# popular tags include #godot, #blender and #madewithgodot; this is Python
# with Pillow, so they are not in the pool. #a11y and #screenreader are in,
# because the alt text really does carry the whole board.
#
# Day-locked tags are the strongest in this crowd (#screenshotsaturday is
# used by 4 of the 63) and the easiest to get wrong — posting it on a Tuesday
# is worse than not posting it at all.
